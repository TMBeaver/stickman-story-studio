The keeper recognized the drawing, but not the afternoon inside the jar. He had sold it years ago, when keeping the shop seemed worth anything.

Over time, he had sold more. Her first song. Her ridiculous jokes. The sound of her coming home. He still knew her name.

But knowing a name, he said, was not the same as remembering a life. He had spent years protecting shelves he could not bear to empty.

He took the pink ribbon off the shelf. For a moment, Milo thought he would hide it. Instead, the keeper broke the seal.

The old man's face crumpled into a smile. Then he unlocked the courtyard gate. Take the cart, he said. Take every jar you can.

Behind them, the loading door slammed open. The buyer saw the escaping cart, grabbed a shelf, and tipped it toward the furnace controls.

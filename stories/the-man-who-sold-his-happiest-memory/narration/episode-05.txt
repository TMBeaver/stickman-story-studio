The dropped jar had given its memory back. Outside, the woman pressed a hand to her mouth, remembering something she had thought was gone.

Milo picked up the glass. It was empty. Breaking a seal released the memory to its original owner. Keeping it sealed kept it trapped.

They could free their two jars right there. Instead, Lena turned toward the shelves. Hundreds of other people's happiest days waited in the dark.

A school concert. A wedding morning. A quiet goodbye. The little tags made each jar sound small. Together, they looked like an entire town.

Then they found the disposal chute. Broken glass went to a furnace. Its warning was simple: a burned memory could never be returned.

Beside the furnace stood their father's auction file. Inside was a hospital invoice. The patient was not their father. The patient was Milo.

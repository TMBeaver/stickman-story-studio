Milo had been sick as a baby. He knew the family story: a long hospital stay, a successful operation, a father who never complained.

What nobody had told him was how the bill got paid. His father had walked into this same shop with nothing left to sell.

Except one thing. The first time his baby had gripped his finger. A tiny hand. A whole new life. His happiest memory.

He traded that moment for the operation. Then he went home and learned to love his son without remembering the moment he first had.

Milo looked at his own sealed jar. Dad had lost a beginning to give him a future. Milo had sold their ending for rent.

He reached for the seal. Footsteps stopped behind them. The buyer was watching. He said the jars were his now, and locked the door.

The first time Milo sold a memory, he asked if it would hurt. The shopkeeper said no. That was the first lie.

His rent was overdue. His drawing commissions had stopped. In his pocket, he had three coins and a key that might soon fit nothing.

The shop bought memories. A school prize paid almost nothing. A first kiss paid more. His happiest afternoon would cover the entire rent.

Milo chose a summer afternoon with his father. Two lemonades. A crooked drawing. His dad laughing so hard he could barely hold the pencil.

A warm light slipped into the jar. Milo took the money. He remembered his father's face, but that afternoon had become an empty space.

That evening, his sister found the receipt. Her smile vanished. Of all the afternoons, she whispered, you sold the last one we had together.

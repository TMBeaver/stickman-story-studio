Their father had sold the moment Milo was born. Lena checked the date twice. It was not a mistake. Neither was the payment.

The amount was enormous. Milo remembered a gentle man who patched his own coat. What could possibly have cost him that much money?

The keeper took the receipt away. Some people sell because they want more, he said. Some sell because someone else needs to live.

Before Milo could answer, a polished black car arrived. A buyer stepped out. The keeper straightened his coat and stopped looking at them.

Lena pulled her brother behind the shelves. Through a narrow doorway, they watched the buyer choose a memory and lower a glass viewing lens.

A stranger's childhood flickered across the wall. The buyer laughed at a father's joke. Milo recognized the crooked drawing. Someone was watching his afternoon.

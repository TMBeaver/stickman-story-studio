Milo had sold his last afternoon with Dad. The next morning, their father had gone into hospital. He had never come home.

He knew those facts. He could repeat them. But he could no longer remember the last time his father had made him laugh.

He ran back with the money. The keeper did not touch it. Sold memories belonged to the auction house until a collector bought them.

At midnight, the jars would leave town. The sales were legal. The buyers were wealthy. And Milo could not afford his own happiness anymore.

Then he saw his jar on a packing cart. Beside it sat an older one, cloudy with dust, carrying his father's name.

Milo reached for the old receipt beneath it. The memory was called My Son's First Breath. His father had sold it twenty years ago.
